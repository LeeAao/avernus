# Tashalar

Capital: Tashluta.

Population: 889,920.

Population by people:

- Humans: 94%.
- Lizardfolk: 4%.
- Yuan-ti: 1%.

Government: merchant oligarchy.

Religions: Chauntea, Malar, Savras, Waukeen.

Imports: beef, fruit, silver, slaves.

Exports: cheese, dyed fabric, glass, olive oil, olives, seafood, spices, wine.

Alignment: CN, N, NE.

More civilized than Chult and more friendly than Calimshan, Tashalar was an exotic land of strange food, beautiful people, and great wealth. Its skilled craftsfolk made excellent crossbows and fast ships. Its accomplished hunters kept trophies of their most dangerous kills.

The people of Tashalar lived along the strip of friendly coastline on the southern Shining Sea, avoiding the Black Jungles and entering the Hazuk Mountains only occasionally to mine gold and iron.

Tashalaran grapes were famous for producing wines valuable even in Waterdeep. This wealth and splendor were shadowed by the Rundeen consortium, which choked trade, and the evil yuan-ti that haunted the jungles.

## Life and Society

The larger towns of Tashalar were effectively small city-states and home to wealthy, ostentatious merchant families. The princes of these families controlled the merchant consortiums that ruled the country.

Common folk who did not work in fields, groves, or vineyards often worked as sailors, shipwrights, sailmakers, or provisioners.

Hunting was a popular sport. The bravest hunters sought out deadly jungle creatures. Spicy food and strong wine were typical dining fare.

The people of Tashalar were preoccupied with the future. Every adult owned at least one set of cards or plaques for personal divination, with wealthier people owning sets made of silver or ivory. They saw the hand of Savras the All-Seeing in daily card readings, numerology, and astrology.

## Major Geographic Features

Tashalar was warm but breezy, with open land covered in vegetable gardens, olive groves, and vineyards.

### Black Jungles

The Black Jungles were home to three large tribes of yuan-ti, one of which had allied with the Rundeen consortium. The Sesehen tribe provided exotic herbs and poisons to the Rundeen in exchange for slaves and transport of pureblood spies to northern ports.

The jungle had connections to the Underdark, and one temple to the snake god of the yuan-ti contained a portal to a similar place in Hlondeth in the Vilhon Reach.

### Hazuk Mountains

The Hazuk Mountains produced enough gold and iron to support Tashalar's economy. Civilized but short-tempered stone giants lived in remote peaks in enormous rock mansions, trading gems for fine wine and knowledge for dragon-turtle meat.

### Mhair Jungles

The Mhair Jungles contained remnants of an old yuan-ti empire in isolated pockets. Bloodflower, a rare herb extinct elsewhere in Faerun, was harvested there and made into a potent healing salve.

The jungles also held wandering groups of wild dwarves, survivors of a lost kingdom, who traded enormous green spinel gems while guarding the secret of where they found them.

## Important Sites

### Tashluta

Tashluta was the capital of Tashalar and the largest of its city-states. It was the common port for visitors, since only there did locals make an effort to learn other languages of Faerun.

Tashlutans enjoyed playing jokes on foreigners, usually involving extremely spicy food. The city banned snakes of all kinds and killed any that were found.

## Regional History

Long ago, the mercantile empire of Tashtan ruled the central part of the Chultan Peninsula. After many battles with Calimshan and many jungle plagues, Tashtan dissolved into smaller nations, the northernmost of which became the collection of city-states known as Tashalar.

Tashalar was a strong mercantile kingdom and protected its borders with fleets of strong ships. Many of these ships fell under the influence of the Rundeen, a consortium of aggressive traders.

The Rundeen reached into Calimshan through partnership with the Knights of the Shield, an organization of spies active in Amn, Calimshan, and Tethyr. Tashalaran vessels sailed to ports in Calimshan and the Lake of Steam, carrying unusual goods onward through Faerun.

## Plots and Rumors

### Fighting for the Crown

Assassinations and blackmail among the ruling merchant princes were believed to be the work of someone trying to install a single noble as figurehead ruler of Tashalar. Many suspected the Rundeen, the yuan-ti, or several less influential merchant families. All sides began hiring mercenaries and adventurers for protection and counterattacks.

### Risen

A long-sunken ship, covered in seaweed and crewed by coral-encrusted skeletal remains, rose from the sea outside Tashluta's harbor and sailed into the docks. Authorities towed the ship to a private area and refused to reveal its contents. The next morning, all the skeletal crew were gone, and several people were later found dead in the streets.
