# Chult

## Geography

Type: Peninsula

Chult lay at the western end of the Chultan Peninsula in southwest Faerun, in the southern Trackless Sea off the coast of Calimshan. During much of the 15th century DR, after the Spellplague, Chult was separated from the mainland and became an island. After the Second Sundering it was rejoined to the continent.

The land remained remote and isolated regardless of its exact coastlines. High mountains, thick jungle, dangerous beasts, dinosaurs, disease, and difficult travel kept most outsiders near the coast. At the same time, Chult was famous for wealth, ruins, gems, gold, ivory, perfume, teak, and other valuable resources, which continually drew adventurers and treasure hunters.

Population in 1372 DR: 440,640.

Government: tribal in rural areas; theocracy in Mezro.

Religions: Eshowdow, Thard Harr, Ubtao.

Alignment: LN, CE, LG.

## Society

Demonym: Chultan.

Approximate population by people:

- Humans: 60%
- Goblins: 20%
- Lizardfolk: 10%
- Wild dwarves: 5%
- Pterafolk: 4%
- Other: 1%

Traditional Chultan human communities lived in tribal groups of a dozen or so families. These groups moved when the poor jungle soil could no longer support them. For hunting and defense they relied mainly on non-metal weapons: clubs, bows, short spears, and similar arms. Iron and steel were rare and arrived mostly through outsiders.

The people of Chult traditionally revered Ubtao almost exclusively. The gods of Faerun had granted Chult to Ubtao as his sole dominion in exchange for his watch over Dendar the Night Serpent, who was believed to be imprisoned beneath the Peaks of Flame.

Arcane magic was generally feared in traditional Chultan communities. A tribal-family typically allowed only one arcane practitioner, who performed hunting ceremonies and brewed potions for hunters. People with arcane talent were no rarer than elsewhere in Faerun, so some fled into the jungle to practice alone, while others traveled to study in Mezro.

Nonhuman inhabitants included wild dwarves, goblins, aldani, pterafolk, bullywugs, and lizardfolk. More exotic creatures such as chuuls, hydras, nagas, troglodytes, trolls, and wyverns existed in smaller numbers. Dinosaurs were the dominant predators, and many Chultans revered them as aspects of Ubtao.

Chult was also known for slimes, molds, carnivorous plants, insects, parasites, and diseases. Travelers risked illnesses from insect bites, jungle mists, parasites, tainted water, and other sources.

### Chultan Weapons

Many wealthy warriors or chiefs owned weapons of good steel acquired from northern traders, but weapons made of stone, wood, obsidian, or copper were not uncommon in Chult.

No Chultan armorers worked in heavy metal armor, due to the scarcity of iron and steel and the hot, humid conditions of the peninsula. Warriors wore hide breastplates and carried large oval hide shields. Chultan hide breastplates were equivalent to leather armor, and hide shields were equivalent to large wooden shields.

Common local weapons included handaxes, bows, clubs, halfspears, bolas, knives, and shortspears.

## Commerce

Imports: food, jewelry, weapons.

Exports: furs, gems, ivory, perfume, teak wood.

Chult attracted safari hunters and collectors of monster skulls. Such hunters sometimes paid others to capture or kill exotic creatures so that the hunters could claim trophies without personally risking the jungle.

## The Jungle

The jungles of Chult were widely considered among the most dangerous places in Faerun. Plants could be intelligent predators, beasts were huge and well-armed with claws and teeth, insects constantly bit and stung, poisonous snakes hid in the undergrowth, and the heat and humidity were severe.

Disease was one of the greatest threats. Many creatures and places in the jungle carried sickness or parasites. Water generally had to be boiled or purified before it was safe to drink. Intelligent jungle creatures such as yuan-ti and batiri could track travelers and hunt them for food.

Despite these hazards, adventurers continued entering the jungle because of rumors of gold, great gems, ruined empires, and lost magical treasures.

Valued trees and plant resources included cork, rubber, and teak.

The famous jungles covered most of the western Chultan Peninsula. Only the highest mountains rose clear of the greenery, though great marshy clearings and grasslands surrounded large lakes in the interior.

Travel in the deep canopy jungle was sometimes easier than travel through denser undergrowth, but it remained dangerous. Beneath hundred-foot trees, where little sun reached the ground, explorers could encounter giant solifugids, ants, spiders, carrion crawlers, purple worms, fungi, vines, and other predators in the gloom.

Foreign explorers sought gold veins, gems as large as a human palm, exotic plants valued by collectors in Amn, Calimshan, Sembia, Tethyr, Thay, and Waterdeep, and sources of poisons, perfumes, and medicines. They also sought huge jungle timbers and rubber trees.

Chult's riches were not considered exaggerated. Satraps of Calimshan sent a steady stream of enslaved people into the jungles to cut roads through vines and reach known mines and ruined cities. Most died, but a few escaped with stories of caves beneath the jungle, gigantic bats or cloakers, ruined cities choked with coins and gems, flying six-headed snakes, the fabled city of Mezro, and deepspawn that vomited living duplicates of creatures they had devoured.

## Peaks of Flame

The Peaks of Flame were inhabited by salamanders and other fiery creatures. Legends placed a pair of iron doors beneath the peaks, said to lead to the Fugue Plane, the realm of the dead. At the end of the world, Dendar the Night Serpent was expected to tear down these doors and break into the world.

## Rivers

The four major rivers of Chult were:

- River Soshenstar, flowing into the Bay of Chult in the north.
- River Tiryki, flowing into the Bay of Chult in the north.
- River Olung, flowing into Kitcher's Inlet on the Shining Sea in the east.
- River Tath, flowing into Jahaka Bay in the south.

Some rivers moved so slowly that the direction of current could be hard to notice, but they became fast where they passed through narrow gorges. River water was not usually safe to drink without boiling or purification, especially because of throat leeches.

River life included Chultan fan shrimp, giant and purple marsh crab, irrinda, kapenta, likoto, minjiriya, mkunga eels, Esmel perch, giant and striped pufferfish, quipper, red discus, Soshenstar barb, tigershell nerite, and giant tigerfish.

## Valley of Lost Honor

Deep in the jungle lay the Valley of Lost Honor, once the last refuge of the Eshowe before they were destroyed. Legends claimed that their wealth remained hidden in caves there. Darker stories said that Eshowdow, actually Shar in disguise, dwelled in the valley, gathering followers and planning revenge.

## Wild Coast

The southwestern coast of Chult was jagged and dangerous. Northern merchants avoided it because of cliffs, whirlpools, sudden storms, aquatic monsters, flying dinosaurs, and giant eagles. Some explorers still searched the coastal caves, hoping they led to rich mines beneath the jungle.

The southern coast extended east to the Snout of Omgar, a mountainous island marking the boundary between Chult and the coast of Samarach. The Snout was associated with the tortle race.

## Important Sites

Fort Beluarian was a small outpost of the Flaming Fist, the mercenary company based in Baldur's Gate. Its troops worked with adventurers and offered shelter in emergencies. Over the Hundred Years of Chaos, locals burned the fort down twice, but Baldurians rebuilt it both times. The fort competed with the much larger Port Nyanzaru.

Mezro was the largest civilized area of Chult and a holy city of the Chultan tribes. It was generally peaceful, and few people besides outside adventurers carried weapons. It was protected by the undying barae of Ubtao and six paladin-like chosen who ruled the city. Until 1363 DR, a magical wall hid Mezro and confused those who approached. After victory against the batiri goblins the protection was lowered, and the city became a haven for explorers until the Spellplague, when it was believed to have collapsed into the earth.

Any chronic lawbreaker in Mezro was tattooed with a blue triangle on the forehead and exiled. Mezro had its own college of wizardry, though it was generally closed to non-Chultans. After studying there for several years, Chultan wizards usually returned to their native tribes. Only rarely did Chultan mages travel to other regions of Faerun to seek their fortunes.

Port Nyanzaru was a harbor port settled by Amnians. It was also a powerful fortress built to withstand pirates and jungle threats. Under Amn, the city became a wealthy and densely populated colony.

In older accounts, Port Nyanzaru's harbor was designed with defense as its first priority because of pirates in nearby waters. It was rumored that the harbormaster paid tribute to a powerful dragon turtle that drove away other sea monsters.

## History

By the late 15th century DR, Chult had enough outside attention that jungle tourism existed. Visitors from Baldur's Gate described the region as too humid and dangerous.

Millennia earlier, Ubtao created the jungle and populated it with creatures. Four thousand years ago he built Mezro with his own hands and empowered the barae, his Chosen, to defend it. Eventually Ubtao tired of the constant demands of the city's people and withdrew, leaving the barae to rule Mezro in his place.

Realizing that creation had distanced him from mortals, Ubtao imbued part of his essence into the land, creating nature spirits and inadvertently drawing the attention of a dark shadow. A human tribe called the Eshowe freed this shadow-creature, Eshowdow, who attacked Mezro and caused great destruction before being driven back.

In retaliation, Ras Nsi, one of the barae, hunted down and killed the entire Eshowe people. Most Chultans later forgot the Eshowe and Ras Nsi's ancient crime.

Several years before the late 14th-century opening of Mezro, the undying King Osaw, one of Ubtao's Chosen, opened the city to the outside world again. Trade goods then flowed between Mezro and the rest of Faerun.

## Rumors and Legends

One local legend concerned the Uluu Thalongh, a mysterious beast said to possess trees and plants, turn branches into weapons, and mimic the voices of its victims. No one discovered what the creature truly was.

Black dragons were rumored to roost in the upper jungle canopy.

Accounts from the far north suggested the possible existence of a surface drow city in Chult's southern jungles, belonging to the lorendrow.

### Dragons of the Coast

Dragon turtles lurked in the sea near river outlets, seeking prey and tribute. Local interests sometimes hired heroes to kill or distract such creatures so that valuable shipments could escape unhindered. Certain wizards also required dragon turtle parts for exotic spells or magic items and paid dearly for carcasses.

### Fallen Champion

The renegade bara Ras Nsi was exiled long ago for his ruthless extermination of the Eshowe. He still maintained a twisted devotion to Mezro and violently disagreed with Osaw's opening of the city. Determined to save Mezro from its rulers, he raised an army of undead to overthrow his former peers and drive all outlanders from Chult.
