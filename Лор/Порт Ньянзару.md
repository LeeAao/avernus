# Port Nyanzaru

## Geography

Aliases: Gateway to Chult.

Type: City.

Region: Chult.

Size: Small city.

Port Nyanzaru was a port city in northern Chult and the major trading center of the region. It stood on the Bay of Chult at the mouths of the River Soshenstar and River Tiryki. The city was built on four hills: Mount Sibasa, Temple Hill, Throne Hill, and Yklwazi Hill.

Ships approaching through the Bay of Chult had to pass through the territory of Aremag, a great dragon turtle that demanded tribute from vessels. By land, the city was less than a day's journey from dangerous swamps and ancient ruins.

Population:

- 9,375 in 1372 DR.
- 12,000 in 1479 DR.

## Society

Main population: Chultan humans, around 67%.

Other inhabitants: around 33%.

Religions: Ubtao, Waukeen, Gond, Savras, Sune, Tymora.

## Politics

Port Nyanzaru was independent in the mid-to-late 14th century DR, later became an Amnian colony, and regained independence sometime in the 1480s DR. In the late 1480s or early 1490s it was ruled by seven merchant princes.

Allegiances:

- Amn in 1479 DR.
- Independent by the late 1480s or early 1490s.

## Wards and Districts

The walled city was divided into three main wards:

- Merchants' Ward, an upscale district.
- Harbor Ward, a busy dockside and shipping district.
- Market Ward, a crowded commercial district.

Three slum districts stood outside the walls:

- Old City, west of the walls.
- Malar's Throat, south of the walls.
- Tiryki Anchorage, southeast of the walls.

## Government

In the mid-14th century DR, the Triceratops Society, also called the Ytepka Society, was the most powerful organization in Port Nyanzaru. It controlled the loading and unloading of all dock cargo, watched visitors arriving by sea, and paid special attention to suspected mages and people carrying magic items. If a suspicious visitor entered the jungle, Society agents warned nearby villages.

## Amnian Trade Colony

Amn asserted colonial control in Chult during the late 14th century DR. By the late 15th century DR, Amnian rulers and trading companies controlled mercantile life in Port Nyanzaru. Taxes were high, and residents were effectively charged for the safety the city provided against the jungle.

People not tied to a major trading company were forced into the shantytowns outside the walls. The trading companies helped protect the city, but they were preoccupied with financial competition, favor with Amnian nobles, and recruiting powerful adventurers.

Active companies included Charisk Expeditionary Outfitters, Naelishti's Naturals, the Order of Belox, Zazefay Logging and Mining, and House Selemchant of Amn.

In 1479 DR, the Amnian governor was a thin human man with a reedy voice and theatrical manner.

## Merchant Princes

Sometime in the 1480s DR, a consortium of Chultan traders backed by the Triceratops Society helped Port Nyanzaru win independence from Amn. The seven richest traders became merchant princes and ruled as a council, with each prince holding an equal vote.

The council could create or change the city's laws, then defer to the judiciary to interpret them. The princes took few daily governing duties directly, preferring to delegate to relatives and agents. Their government was loose and somewhat inefficient, but the Triceratops Society supported it by identifying threats to the princes' rule.

The princes' power came from wealth and trade influence. They used private fortunes to manage foreign relationships and profitable alliances, in ways similar to the Amnian statecraft they had learned from. They did not have enough military power to control the jungles or contest the Flaming Fist at nearby Fort Beluarian, which claimed control over much of Chult in the late 15th century DR. Their direct influence was mostly limited to Port Nyanzaru and some lumber and mining operations near the Bay of Chult.

In the Old City, their influence was reduced by the beggar princes, who controlled local thieves through extortion and threats.

Goldenthrone was the palace and seat of the merchant princes. They held audiences there and met subjects or official visitors, while each prince managed private business from a personal villa.

The merchant princes strengthened their position by claiming control over the sale and export of key goods and services. Any sale of goods or services under a prince's monopoly but outside that prince's oversight was treated as black market dealing.

The seven merchant princes of the late 1480s or early 1490s were:

- Ekene-Afa, who controlled weapons, shields, rain catchers, canoes, saddles, wagons, and traveling gear.
- Ifan Talro'a, who controlled beasts and beast training, especially dinosaurs.
- Jessamine, who controlled plants, herbs, poisons, and assassination sanctions.
- Jobal, who controlled guides and sellswords.
- Kwayothe, who controlled fruit, wine, ale, tej, insect repellent, oil, perfume, and similar goods.
- Wakanga O'tamu, who controlled knowledge, lore, magic items, potions, scrolls, and magical services.
- Zhanthi, who controlled gems, jewelry, cloth, armor made from nonmetal materials, and dyed goods.

## Trade

Port Nyanzaru was the center of Chultan trade. Its goods and services included jungle supplies, dinosaurs, animals, textiles, gems, jewels, plants, poisons, potions, scrolls, magic items, lumber, perfume, oil, alcohol, and guide services.

The Grand Souk was the city's famous mercantile heart. The Red Bazaar served local daily needs. The Jewel Market handled gems and luxury goods. Hunter's Square was a gathering place for adventurers and hunters.

Most foreign trade took place in the Grand Souk, while locals made more use of the Red Bazaar. Because the sale of many goods was controlled by the merchant princes' monopolies, foreign traders had to work through the princes' agents in order to trade legally. Ships arriving in port were inspected, sometimes with the Harbormaster's magic, and customs agents could confiscate trade goods.

Major exports included jewels, gold, timber, rubber-tree products, exotic plants, poisons, perfumes, medicines, and colorful textile dyes. Gem-cutting, jewelry-making, dye works, and apothecaries were important local trades. Foreigners were commonly offered equipment, tracking services, old Serpentes relics, and maps of doubtful accuracy.

## Law and Order

The merchant princes were able to shape law, but judges interpreted it with flexibility. Port Nyanzaru's courts had a reputation for being harder to bribe than many foreigners expected.

Executioner's Run was a public punishment in the Old City. Condemned criminals were thrown into a pit with beasts and could win freedom and fame by surviving.

Licensed assassination was possible through sanctions sold by Jessamine. Such documents allowed the killing of a specific target in a specified manner, often with a blade or poison.

Sanctions cost at least 150 gold pieces, with the price rising depending on the motive, the target's status, and the buyer's means. No more than six sanctions were usually sold in a given year, and many people avoided buying them for fear of retaliation by sanction.

Slavery was not fully illegal, but slavers were unwelcome in the city and could not deal with the merchant princes.

## Culture

Port Nyanzaru was loud, colorful, and commercial. Bright lightweight clothing was common, including long shirts, knee-length kilts, trousers, and sarongs in blue, green, orange, red, and yellow.

Cleanliness was culturally important. Residents used public baths, private baths, the temple of Sune, or frequent rain to stay clean.

### Food and Drink

The recreational alcohol of choice was tej. Popular foods in the late 15th century DR included atkilt wat, asa wat, crema Athkatla, doro t'ibs, kinche, and kita.

Food relied heavily on fish and seafood. Fish and other seafood were staples and were sold at a fish market in the Market Ward, where the selection was usually best in the morning. Fishers preferred the sea over the rivers because it was safer to stay away from the jungle. Reptile meat was also eaten, but it was considered tough and less desirable than fish.

### Entertainment

The people of Port Nyanzaru enjoyed loud, wild music and believed in savoring life. The city had a strong literary and theater scene, including many active book clubs. By the late 15th century DR, formulaic romantic adventure stories were popular, especially tales in which heroes saved lovers from danger or terrible fates.

Entertainment included gladiatorial events, betting, Executioner's Run, weekly dinosaur races, and the annual Running of the Saurs. During major events that brought many foreigners into the city, temporary outdoor parade grounds and markets were assembled.

### Religion

Like the rest of Chult, Port Nyanzaru originally worshiped Ubtao, protector and patron of Chult. By the 14th century DR, worship of Savras had also gained influence because Savras's priests helped track the difficult-to-read activities of yuan-ti enemies.

After Ubtao's apparent abandonment of Chult at the end of the 14th century DR, many Chultans of Port Nyanzaru accepted northern gods brought by missionaries. Waukeen became especially popular among the merchant class; Gond among artisans, crafters, and engineers; Savras through one of the first great temples to a foreign god in the city; Sune because of her emphasis on beauty and cleanliness; and Tymora among vulnerable lower-class residents outside the walls who relied on luck and the defenses of the Tymoran temple against jungle monsters.

Other gods recognized in the city included Kossuth, Malar, and Umberlee.

By the late 15th century DR, hostility toward foreign missionaries had developed because they were seen as exploiting the local people. The city's priests were therefore mostly local Chultans, even when the gods they served were foreign.

## Defenses

Port Nyanzaru's fortifications included city walls, gates, Fort Nyanzaru, harbor defenses, and a lighthouse. The city was built to withstand pirates, sea attack, and threats from the jungle.

The inner city was protected by a high stone wall. The slums outside the walls remained comparatively undefended. Additional walls within the city protected wealthy and important areas. The walls were made of fitted stone and decorated with colorful geometric patterns and images of beasts and heroes.

The harbor was defended by Fort Nyanzaru, which controlled a massive iron chain across the harbor entrance. The chain could be raised to stop ships entering or leaving. In case of sea attack, bells in the fort were rung and a red beacon was lit atop the harbor lighthouse.

In the late 15th century DR, the city had no standing army. The merchant princes paid a large force of foreign mercenaries, many of them Zhentarim troops, to man the walls, patrol the streets, watch the markets, and guard their interests. These guards did little to protect people outside the walls. Residents of the outer districts organized their own volunteer Citizens' Brigade for defense.

Warning horns were used during attacks to tell people outside the walls to flee through the gates. Because of the hot and humid climate, defenders rarely wore heavy armor, preferring leather or dinosaur-hide shields and armor. By the late 15th century DR, there was growing interest in lightweight titansteel armor.

Rain traps on many buildings made fires easy to put out.

## History

### Ancient History

A lost civilization once occupied the site that became Port Nyanzaru. It built the great ziggurats of the Old City, as well as dungeons, catacombs, and ruins beneath the Old City, Harbor Ward, and Tiryki Anchorage. These ruins often contained traps and were marked by wavy or circular carvings, indecipherable glyphs, and images of Chultans laboring or hunting each other.

Little was known of this civilization except that its kings possessed powerful magic capable of inspiring whole armies and that they practiced human sacrifice to dark gods. The kings who ordered the ziggurats built were said to have had their architects buried alive with them to serve in death, perhaps embalmed and placed in sarcophagi within or beneath the ziggurats.

At some point, a temple to Umberlee existed in caves beneath the Harbor Ward, but it was abandoned.

### 1300s DR

By the mid-14th century DR, Port Nyanzaru had become the most important trade hub in Chult, a position it kept even after Mezro ended its isolation in 1363 DR. It was believed, though not confirmed, that the harbormaster of this period paid monthly tribute to a powerful dragon turtle in exchange for keeping other sea monsters away from the port and protecting trade.

When Amnian rule began in the late 14th century DR, Port Nyanzaru was used to extract resources for foreign rulers. People unaffiliated with Amnian trading companies were discriminated against and barred from living inside the city walls, leaving them exposed to jungle dangers and diseases.

After the Spellplague in 1385 DR, Port Nyanzaru was the only major settlement left in Chult after the disappearance of Mezro. This remained true through the late 15th century DR.

### 1400s DR

In the 15th century DR, the Pyramid of the Moon appeared within sight of the city walls for one night, then vanished by morning along with more than two hundred people from Port Nyanzaru.

In 1479 DR, Amnian trading companies hosted a great hunt that drew adventurers from across Faerun with prize offers. Officially the hunt was meant to reduce dangerous fauna near the city; unofficially it was a competition among trading companies to sponsor the strongest hunters and kill the most impressive monsters.

Amn withdrew from Port Nyanzaru in the late 15th century DR after pressure from powerful merchants and the recognition that holding the city would likely lead to a bloody conflict Amn could not win.

By the late 1480s DR, vegepygmy activity outside the city increased. Port Nyanzaru's government paid adventurers to reduce vegepygmy numbers and slow their expansion.

Nine years after independence from Amn, the death curse brought many adventurers to Port Nyanzaru in search of its source. At the same time, yuan-ti cultists of Dendar carried out schemes to undermine the city. These plots were secretly directed by Kress'voon, a yuan-ti anathema. One plot involved smuggling the Dreamer's Amulet into the city, causing factions to suspect it was tied to the death curse. Another involved poisoning dinosaur racers with Wanderlost to disrupt the betting economy. Kress'voon's threat persisted after the end of the death curse: he sought to recreate the curse under his own control and use it to summon Dendar to the Prime Material Plane with the aid of the lich Rhaugilath.

## Inhabitants

Port Nyanzaru was inhabited mostly by humans of Chultan descent. A few tabaxi lived in the city, working as minstrels or guides. Chultan shield dwarves also lived in the port, including albino dwarves. Yuan-ti spies resided in Port Nyanzaru as well, watching the merchant princes, treasure hunters, and adventurers.

### Notable Inhabitants

In the late 15th century DR, notable inhabitants aside from the merchant princes included:

- Alastar Bol, the "Fat Knight", ambassador of the Order of the Gauntlet.
- Pock-Marked Po, the "Hideous Prince" of the Old City, with ties to the Zhentarim.
- Screaming Wind, a tabaxi hunter and member of the Emerald Enclave.
- Sibonseni, chief priest of Waukeen.
- Soggy Wren, the ranking Harper in the city and a notorious drunkard.
- Tearah Initoo, the Master of the Market.

- Harbormaster Zindar, a half-gold dragon and the city's harbormaster.
