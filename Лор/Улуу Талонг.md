# Uluu Thalongh

Of all the legends whispered about the monster-infested, swamp-riddled jungles of Chult, one of the eeriest was the Uluu Thalongh, a name whose meaning had been lost along with the tribe that named it.

The Uluu Thalongh was described as a huge, ancient, malevolent flesh-eating beast. A weird, tuneless piping always marked its approach. The sound drove intelligent jungle creatures into headlong flight or mortal fear.

When it closed on intended prey, the Uluu went within jungle plants and trees, causing them to ripple and bulge. These swellings moved swiftly to branches, then burst forth as huge jaws at the ends of tentacles. These eyeless jaws could see prey perfectly, snapping and tearing until all nearby creatures died or fled.

If one of these jaws was killed, the Uluu left the area, but the creature itself was not slain. Even cabals of wizards using fiery and explosive spells to obliterate miles of jungle failed to destroy the Uluu Thalongh.

More frightening was its tendency to lure prey by causing other beings to speak for it. To create a speaker, the Uluu first stung a jungle creature or human by driving a rootlet into the target. Through this rootlet, it sucked out all nutrients, leaving the speaker to crumble into dust as an empty husk.

The Uluu could then convey its thoughts and mental control through the speaker. Even creatures that normally could not speak could hoarsely whisper words to lure humans to its jaws. Sometimes speakers pleaded for help or shouted false cries for aid from afar. More often, they posed as aides or companions whom the Uluu had already slain.

Who or what the Uluu Thalongh truly was remained a mystery.
