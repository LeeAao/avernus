# Amn

Amn, also known as the Merchant's Domain, was a tremendously wealthy nation in West Faerun. Before the Spellplague and Maztica's disappearance, Amn held colonies far away in Maztica. After the Spellplague, it still controlled important trade routes to Calimshan and Baldur's Gate, as well as colonial ports in the Moonshaes and Chult.

Its population, mostly human with a large minority of halflings, was historically around three million. The country's natural resources, wealth, and mercantile nature made it one of Faerun's most important and influential trading centers. Its capital, Athkatla, founded in 100 DR, was known as the City of Coin.

## Geography

Aliases:

- The Merchant's Domain.
- Formerly: Calishar Emirates.

Region: Lands of Intrigue.

Capital: Athkatla.

Geographically, Amn was a fairly small state, though still large compared with Baldur's Gate. Historically, it held several colonies in Maztica; these disappeared from Toril with the rest of the continent during the Spellplague. Amn remained a colonial power through the 15th century DR, mainly through fortified ports in Chult and the Moonshae Isles, as well as the Mhair Archipelago.

### Climate

The weather in Amn was pleasant almost all year. Around Lake Esmel, temperatures were warmer because of hot springs. A diverse selection of crops grew well in Amn, creating a surplus traded with other nations.

Amn's rainy season ran from Uktar to early Tarsakh. Winters were mild, with minor freezes and a little snow across most of the nation except Esmeltaran on Lake Esmel. Rivers froze during Hammer, but the ice did not harden enough to cross safely. Winter blizzards in the Cloud Peaks could isolate Nashkel from Athkatla.

### Geographical Features

#### Bodies of Water

- Lake Esmel: a lake on Amn's eastern border, unusually deep, with several hot mineral springs and, according to local legend, a powerful dragon.

#### Forests

- Forest of Shadows: also known as Shilmista, an ancient woodland overlapping with the elven kingdom of Elbereth.
- Snakewood.

#### Mountains

- Cloud Peaks: a mountain range at Amn's northern edge, infested with dangerous monsters and containing precious gems.
- Mount Speartop: the highest point in Amn, three miles above sea level.
- Small Teeth: a mountain range containing many evil humanoids.
- Snowflake Mountains: a mountain range in West Faerun.
- Troll Mountains: low mountains named for intelligent trolls living on their peaks. A huge four-thousand-foot slab resembling a dwarf stood there, marking a long-abandoned dwarven city. The mountains were also notable for large gem deposits.

## Politics

Type: oligarchical nation state.

Meisarch: Selemchant.

Legislative body: Council of Five.

Judicial authority: Tessarch.

Since its unification under Thayze Selemchant, Amn was ruled by the Council of Six in Athkatla. The council's main duties were maintaining trade within and beyond Amn, controlling nearly every economic aspect of the nation, and coordinating military defense. Council seats were held anonymously.

After the Spellplague, the number of council seats was reduced from six to five. Amn remained an oligarchy but stopped pretending to be otherwise. The positions were openly held by rulers of five houses: Alibakkar, Dannihyr, Nashivaar, Ophal, and Selemchant.

Many other forces still held power in Amn despite the Council's attempts to contain them. Directly below the Council were the head merchant families, from whose ranks new council members were elected. Families without council seats had little direct governmental power, but could still be very influential in their towns and cities. In any Amnian city, ten to thirty influential families might dominate local life; Athkatla had forty-two families controlling the nation's trading houses.

Below them were mercantile houses: companies formed by consolidating wealth from lesser merchants or merchant families. Both the Council and the Shadow Thieves supported these consortia with small investments in order to monitor the interests of guilds and lesser families.

Beside the merchant houses stood the Cowled Wizards, the Emerald Cabal, and the Shadow Thieves. The Cowled Wizards were the only legal arcane institution in Amn, all others being banned by Council decree. They were controlled by the Selemchants and often served as underhanded agents or mercenaries. The Emerald Cabal was a secretive league of illegal arcane spellcasters opposed to the Amnian system and willing to use violence. The Shadow Thieves, though illegal, held considerable power through a criminal network flowing between their lair in Crimmor, the rest of Amn, and the Sword Coast.

## History

Established: 1333 DR.

1370-1374 DR: Sothillisian War.

Preceded by: Athkatla.

Succeeded by: extant.

Amn was one of Faerun's youngest nations and one of its richest. By the time of the Spellplague, it had expanded farther than any other Faerunian power, reaching across the Trackless Sea to the shores of Maztica. When Maztica disappeared during the Year of Blue Fire, Amn lost that colonial sphere.

Amn's status as a colonial power was not broken by this loss. In the absence of its old colonies, the rulers of the Merchant's Domain opened colonies in more local arenas, including Chult, the Mhair Archipelago, and the Moonshae Isles.

## Government and Offices

### Village Lords and Harbormasters

In the Lands of Intrigue, including Amn and Tethyr, village lords, also known as mayors, and harbormasters were elective offices in smaller settlements and port townships.

In bigger cities, nobles and wealthy influential locals assigned these positions as reward postings for investors and distinguished individuals.

For elections, voting rights were granted to stakeholders, businesspeople, property owners, and warehouse or shipyard landlords. Votes were cast vocally and in person before the local herald, witnessed by clergy. Candidates could be any local resident.

Vote counts were performed with pebbles tossed into labeled jars. Each stone represented one vote and was dropped as the voter voiced their choice. The jars were safeguarded by the herald and witness priests. Elections could last up to a tenday, but more often took two or three days. Majority ruled. If tampering was detected, a new election was held. If an office remained vacant through repeated elections, local clergy filled its duties until a winner was declared.

### Foreign Relations

Some southern settlements in Amn had divided loyalties, with some preferring to join prosperous Tethyr to the south. Around 1370 DR, the border between Amn and Tethyr was the Tethir Road, though some claimed it should be the Tejarn Hills.

To the east, Amn extended to Shilmista; to the west, to the Sea of Swords. Muranndin, the monster kingdom, posed a major threat to Amnian trade from the south and waged destructive war on Amn during the 1370s, enslaving thousands of Amnians.

To the north, Amn extended to the northern edge of the Cloud Peaks and Nashkel. Historically, Amn had a strong rivalry with Baldur's Gate, nearly resulting in war during the Iron Crisis of the 1360s. More recently, Amn's attention turned toward Waterdeep, its chief rival in international trade.

Amn had few friends. Even Tethyr, nominally an ally, viewed Amn's activities with deep concern.

### Heraldry

Amnian battle banners were usually shaped like golden keystones. Fortress pennants were long, thin stretched triangles. Banners and pennants had bright red bodies and golden borders, with the image of "the Pride of Amn", a badge used on city gates and worn by civic building guards.

"The Pride of Amn" remained unchanged through the century after the Spellplague: a horizontal row of six equal gold circles above a steel-gray open fanged maw. The circles symbolized the "coins of all" and the Council of Six. Their lack of features stressed Amn's acceptance of coinage from across the Realms and the anonymity of the Council. The maw was called the daerag and symbolized Amn devouring its trade adversaries.

## Trade

Money flowed in and out of Amn like in no other country. Goods from Calimshan and Tethyr passed through Amn on their way to the Heartlands. Amn rivaled Waterdeep and Sembia.

Amn drew merchants, businesspeople, and adventurers from many lands, even from outside Faerun.

Because of Amn's wealth and dependence on smooth trade, merchant companies, often run by wealthy families or houses, held enormous power and could compete with traditional political forces.

In the past, stone was imported from Mirabar via Luskan for construction, transported by expensive magic. After Luskan's demise, that route was cut off. Amn's commercial lifeline was constantly threatened by Muranndin to the south.

## Demographics

About one sixth of Amn's population lived in cities and major towns. The rest lived in villages and rural farmland. Two thirds of the population lived west of Lake Esmel.

Around the Year of Wild Magic, roughly 84% of inhabitants were human and 15% were halflings. The halfling population grew substantially in the 15th century DR because of the destruction of Luiren, the ancestral halfling homeland.

Native humans of Amn were primarily descended from Calishites and Tethyrians. Shield dwarves lived in small numbers in the mountains, wild elves in Shilmista or Snakewood, and few of either race settled in cities. Gnomes were rare in cities and drew interest. Half-orcs and half-elves were rare, except in Purskul, where more than 20% of the labor force was half-orc. Half-orcs were treated as second-class citizens.

### Halflings

In Amnian society, humans treated halflings as equals because of their business skills. Human businesses tended to follow strict hierarchy and leadership, while halfling businesses relied more on teamwork and shared responsibility.

Few halflings lived west of Esmeltaran, preferring segregation in that city or in Riatavin, both near the lost halfling realm of Meiritin.

## Society

### Culture

Money was the key to status in Amn. A successful person was measured by displayed wealth and material possessions. It was possible to buy nobility for the right price. Lavish parties marked business success and demonstrated status. Expensive gifts and gratuities also displayed rank.

Status could also be measured by the location of a person's property, though the amount of land mattered less than where it was. Athkatla was the most coveted place for a residence, and the Gem District was considered the best district in the city. Rising merchant families were expected to own more than one property, and estates around Lake Esmel were among the finest.

Attire was another sign of status. Women often wore turbans, with more ornate examples showing higher status through gold, silver, or platinum thread. Metal-embroidered silk garments were popular among all classes and genders. Fur vests or cloaks from the North were fashionable despite Amn's temperate climate. Jewelry also displayed rank. Gem-inlaid precious metal rings were common, and pearls, especially black pearls, were highly desired. Among the wealthiest families, overly fancy garments and jewelry were frowned upon; those families preferred simpler dress and other displays of status.

Status was measured using a scale of precious metals, from ore at the bottom to mithral at the top:

- Ore: the lowest rank, meaning filthy or criminal and used as a strong insult.
- Bronze: the working class; also used for a low-rank impostor.
- Copper: acceptable working class.
- Steel: the best of the working class or military.
- Silver: a rising merchant or someone with much potential.
- Gold: one whose wealth was inherited, or who was wealthy without business sense.
- Platinum: the head of a mercantile house.
- Adamantine: the highest self-made rank.
- Mithral: perfection, the highest possible rank.

In general, the richest commanded the less wealthy. A recent show of wealth, such as a major purchase or high bid, could immediately raise a person's status even above those who were wealthier overall. Proper business conduct mattered more than racial stereotypes, so a customer who followed the rules could be accepted regardless of origin. Even so, wealthy Amnians were wary of threats to business and often traveled with at least two guards.

### Languages

Most languages could be heard in Amn because of traders from across the Realms. Thorass was used for official documents and speeches, even though Common served that role in neighboring lands. A regional language sometimes called Amnian common was spoken by rural Amnians; it was a dialect of Common with certain Thorass words.

Amn also had unique common phrases known as Amnian phrases.

### Magic

Arcane magic was illegal in Amn. Only wizards with the patronage of one of the Council of Five could practice their art. Divine magic was not considered a problem as long as the caster was connected to a recognized religious sect.

### Religion

There was no state religion in Amn. Any religious practice was tolerated if it did not interfere with the Council of Six. Religious freedom was important to Amnians, and all religions were represented, though some were more favored because their portfolios matched daily life.

- Waukeen: the premier deity in Amn. Her temple services were often parties and banquets.
- Lliira: her worship grew in Amn because she oversaw Waukeen's portfolios during the Time of Troubles.
- Sune: well suited to Amn because of the importance of status and appearance. Her clergy were often more provocatively dressed there than elsewhere in the Realms.

Helm and Ilmater were not worshiped extensively, except in Trademeet.

### Colonies

- New Amn: the Maztican colony and its capital Helmsport, founded in 1361 DR by the Golden Legion, which enslaved, killed, and integrated natives.
