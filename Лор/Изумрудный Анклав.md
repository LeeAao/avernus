# Emerald Enclave

## Basic Information

Aliases:

- Caretakers.
- Nature's Chosen.
- The Circle.
- Chosen of Silvanus.

Base of operations: Ilighon, Vilhon Reach.

Leaders: the Elder Circle.

Favored deities: Eldath, Mielikki, Silvanus.

Membership alignment: lawful neutral, chaotic neutral, or neutral.

Allegiances: Church of Eldath, Church of Mielikki, Church of Nobanion, Church of Silvanus.

Enemies: Church of Bane, Church of Talos, Church of Malar, People of the Black Blood, Red Wizards of Thay, Zhentarim.

The Emerald Enclave was an organization of druids and other nature worshipers based on the island of Ilighon off the coast of the Vilhon Reach. It was known by many names, including Caretakers, Nature's Chosen, the Circle, and the Chosen of Silvanus.

Despite the power of the Vilhonese nations, as of the 14th century DR those nations did little involving land and nature without at least tacit approval from the Enclave.

## Beliefs

According to the Elder Circle, the Greatest Tenets Number Three were:

- The order of nature must be preserved in all its forms.
- Any force that would disrupt that order had to be defeated.
- The Enclave would always provide aid to anyone left fatigued or injured.

## Organization

The Emerald Enclave was renowned for benevolent deeds and feared for swift action against those responsible for crimes against nature. In the Vilhon Reach, gossip about the Enclave's deeds gave it an almost mythical reputation. Its vindictive ways became cautionary tales in areas bordering forests.

The Enclave was not exclusively druidic. Its membership included bards and other beings who venerated nature and neutrality. No member could follow the paths of good or evil, but apart from that narrow path between light and darkness, the Enclave imposed few restrictions.

### Ranks

From lowest to highest:

- Springwarden.
- Summerstrider.
- Autumnreaver.
- Winterstalker.
- Master of the Wild.

### Hierarchy

The Enclave was arranged into seven circles, each with different duties and a different position in the hierarchy. Each member belonged to one circle and could move between circles through experience and prominence.

The Circle of Initiates included new members. The Circle of Earth welcomed members of the Circle of Initiates as they advanced. The Circle of Air usually included those who had learned wild shape; non-druids could be promoted to the Circle of Air after outstanding service.

From the Circle of Air, members could move into Eldath's Circle, the Circle of Earth, or the Planar Circle.

Eldath's Circle did not necessarily mean worshiping Eldath, but all of its members protected the waterways of Toril and searched for useful information.

The Planar Circle consisted of people whose calling lay beyond the Prime Material plane. It was one of the most respected roles. Many of its members died on other planes or chose never to return to Toril, but continued sending information back to the Enclave.

Members of the Circle of Earth were travelers. They crossed Faerun or the Vilhon Reach as scouts and spies, watching kingdoms, organizations, and politics that could interfere with natural balance.

The Elder Circle, or Elder Circle of Three, governed the Emerald Enclave and was the highest circle. It consisted of only three members at a time: the most powerful druids or religious leaders.

## Dogma

Most members venerated Eldath, goddess of singing waters; Mielikki, lady of the forest; or Silvanus, the Treefather.

## Activities

The Enclave's goals were sixfold.

First, members swore to protect nature in all forms and were willing to give their lives to preserve balance. This did not make every lumberjack an enemy; the Enclave demanded responsible logging, replanting, and careful selection of logging areas.

Second, the Enclave sought to control human expansion. Nature's resources were finite, and not all areas needed development, farms, cities, or roads. The Enclave also enforced strict hunting regulations in the Shining Plains, including the official late-summer and fall hunting season.

Third, the Enclave believed everything in nature had its place and that nature included more than forests. Plains, mountains, rivers, and forests all mattered. Anyone reshaping nature for profit was to be opposed.

Fourth, magic had to be controlled and never used for mass destruction. This creed was adopted after the Rotting Wars.

Fifth, the Enclave warned against magic on a grand scale, considering it a disaster waiting to happen.

Finally, the Enclave presented a united front to outsiders. Internal disagreements were voiced only within Oakenhall on Ilighon. Public discord was considered disrespectful to the Elder Circle and damaging to the organization.

After the Spellplague, the Enclave held that the natural order had to be respected and preserved; forces that would upset it should be destroyed; elemental forces had to be kept in check; wilderness and civilization had to be prevented from destroying one another; and those unable to survive the wilderness should be helped.

### Abilities

All members were taught a unique language called druidspeak, which had some similarities to thieves' cant. It was never taught to outsiders and usually took at least a year to learn.

Members of the Elder Circle received divine gifts from the Enclave's patron gods. They were hidden from magical sight, constantly protected as if by nondetection, unaffected by charm, indoctrination, and mind control, and immune to mundane and magical diseases and afflictions such as lycanthropy. They also aged only one year per decade and needed to sleep only one night out of ten.

Eldathyn Elders could breathe underwater and travel to the Elemental Plane of Water at will. Mielikki's faithful could shapechange into treants and heal when changing back. Silvanites could meld into stone and travel to the Elemental Plane of Earth at will.

Members who sought to master wilderness survival reportedly traveled to Wavecrest, an untamed jungle island neighboring Ilighon. The Enclave did not share its knowledge of Wavecrest with outsiders.

Divine spellcasters of the Enclave who worshiped Silvanus, Mielikki, and Eldath had access to unique spells, including faith-magic zone, re-target, and windlance.

### Initiation

Initiates had to serve neither good nor evil, serve no other organization apart from the churches of Eldath, Mielikki, or Silvanus, be innocent of crimes against nature, and have performed at least one significant deed benefiting nature in the Vilhon Reach.

Initiation rites were held each full moon at Oakenhall, the House of Silvanus at the heart of Ilighon.

## Tactics

The Emerald Enclave followed a pattern when dealing with violators of nature. First, it issued a simple warning. If the warning was ignored, the Enclave acted swiftly and decisively, stopping the wrongdoers by any means.

## Base of Operations

The heart of the Emerald Enclave was the House of Silvanus, or Oakenhall, built atop the mountain range at the center of Ilighon.

Ilighon itself was guarded by a circular reef called Eldath's Ring, and its surrounding waters were guarded by Enclave allies from the Elemental Plane of Water.

By the late 14th century DR, the Emerald Enclave had expanded beyond the Vilhon Reach to the rest of Faerun. To protect forests likely to face danger from expanding civilization, it formed splinter cells in the High Forest, Cormanthor, the Wealdath, and the Great Dale. Older established cells existed in the Chondalwood and Winterwood.

## Possessions

Members did not wear uniforms. Instead, each wore some green article of clothing or jewelry, most often a green pendant or emerald earrings. High-ranking druids could be recognized by eyes that turned bright green and shimmered with Silvanus's holy symbol.

## Relationships

The Emerald Enclave had long-standing conflicts with the Red Wizards of Thay, with mages of the Zhentarim, and with other wizards because of fears that spellcasters would abuse the Art and unleash disasters like the Rotting Wars.

It opposed the churches of Bane, Talos, and Malar, as well as the Malarite cult known as the People of the Black Blood.

The only settlement on Ilighon, Sapra, received only limited land from the Enclave, preventing overgrowth and overpopulation. The town's existence depended on the Enclave's goodwill.

Cities of the Vilhon Reach operated logging industries with the Enclave's permission. Hlath's lumber industry was carefully monitored. Reth's logging was being phased out by 1372 DR after years of tension with the Enclave. Gildenglade was on good terms with the Enclave before its destruction because its elven population handled negotiations, forestry, and forest preservation. The lizardfolk of Surkh maintained cordial relations with the Enclave.

The Enclave protected creatures of the Shining Plains. Its representatives attended the annual memorial on the Hill of Memories for the tragedy of the Field of Tears in 326 DR, and the leaders discussed yearly issues afterward. The Ten-paw, the most powerful clan of the Shining Plains, had an Emerald Enclave representative as an advisor living among the wemic clan.

## History

### Founding

Many considered 374 DR, the Year of the Thoughtful Man, to be the founding year of the Emerald Enclave. More precisely, that was when the Church of Silvanus built a shrine on Ilighon during Midsummer Night. The shrine was called Oakenhome, or the House of Silvanus.

It began as a small druidic conclave for those who mastered shapechanging and could reach the island as birds. The temple was completed a few years later. The druids of Ilighon quietly watched civilization and increasing ship traffic in the Vilhon Reach.

In 717 DR, the Year of the Druid's Wrath, the druids of Oakenhome joined the Emerald Enclave, which eventually made Oakenhome its center.

Turmish began conflicting with the Enclave as early as 552 DR. The first event that established the Enclave as a major power was its battle against Turmish and the Windlass in 717 DR. Turmish sent an army to the druidic sanctuary of Cedarsproke and a war fleet to Ilighon. Giant allies of the Enclave ambushed the troops in the Orsraun Mountains, while water elementals destroyed the Turmishan fleet. This defeat allowed the Enclave to expand into Chondalwood and Winterwood.

The Enclave's early goal was to protect natural resources and interests in the Vilhon Reach from despoilers. One such despoiler was the mohrg warlord Borran Klosk, a servant of Malar who led an undead army in 1150 DR. The Enclave allied with dwarves of Alaoreum at the Battle of Morningstar Hollows, where its druids flooded the Alaoreum River and washed away Borran Klosk's undead. Borran survived and was imprisoned by the Church of Eldath under Alaghon.

In 1248 DR, the town of Ironcloak in Turmish received logging rights but began reckless cutting and polluted the Alaoreum River. After warnings were ignored and an Enclave druid was murdered, the Enclave issued a final warning. At dawn, earth elementals emerged from the forest floor and killed everyone they found. The river flooded its banks and destroyed the town, its lord, and his fortune.

In 1344 DR, the Enclave clashed with Sespech when government-sponsored lumberjacks entered Chondalwood. Sespechian foragers later met Enclave druids and were turned away without bloodshed.

During the Time of Troubles in 1358 DR, Malar's avatar entered Gulthmere Forest to destroy members of the Emerald Enclave. Nobanion's avatar stood with the Enclave against Malar, resulting in Malar's defeat and the removal of the Beastlord's cult from the region for a time.

In 1360 DR, the wizard Danirro of Alaghon attempted to stop the inevitable eruption of Mount Kolimnis. Emerald Enclave agents pushed him into the volcano before he could complete the ritual. His ring of feather falling prolonged his fall, and he was destroyed by lava. His laboratory burned, and records of the spell were lost. The Enclave warned Gildenglade not to meddle with forces it did not understand. Mount Kolimnis continued steaming and did not erupt for another century.

In 1370 DR, Borran Klosk was freed and threatened the Vilhon Reach again with an undead army of drowned ones from the Whamite Isles. His plot was stopped by Haarn Brightoak, a druid of the Enclave. Around the same year, the Whamite Isles suffered an epidemic of mass suicides caused by unknown magic; the Enclave sent ships, avoided the magical dangers, and claimed the islands.

### Post-Spellplague

After the Spellplague of 1385 DR, the Sea of Fallen Stars drained, joining Ilighon to the mainland and to Turmish. This change twisted the organization into a xenophobic force bent on destroying all manifestations of the Spellplague. Much of the order died or left for Gulthandor.

The remaining druids, led by the increasingly mad Cindermoon, formerly Shadowmoon Crystalembers, were less capable and experienced and hated the spellscarred. Agents were sent to plaguelands south of Turmish, and the organization tried to stop pilgrimages through the scarred lands, often violently.

In 1423 DR, Mount Kolimnis finally erupted, left untamed because of the Enclave's earlier intervention. Gildenglade and its 50,000 inhabitants were buried under ash and hot mud.

### The Second Sundering

The Enclave was revitalized in 1486 DR, when Stedd Whitehorn, Chosen of Lathander, cured Cindermoon of Shar-induced madness and the Great Rains refilled the Sea of Fallen Stars.

Along with other factions, the Enclave rose against the Cult of the Dragon's plot to return Tiamat to the mortal realm.
